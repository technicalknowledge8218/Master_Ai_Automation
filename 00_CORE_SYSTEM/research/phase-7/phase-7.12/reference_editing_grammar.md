## 2. Directorial Transition Grammar

### Approved Transition Types in Short-Form

1. **Hard Cut (Motivated)**:
   - Primary cut (85% of transitions). Must coincide with a vocal cadence shift, question/answer turn, or dramatic reveal.
2. **J-Cut (Audio Leads Video)**:
   - The next speaker's voice or incoming sound effect starts **0.15s to 0.30s BEFORE** the visual cuts. Creates anticipation and smooths visual disruption.
3. **L-Cut (Video Leads Audio)**:
   - The visual cuts to the listener's reaction while the previous speaker's voice finishes lingering over the cut. Essential for comedy reactions and suspense.
4. **Match Cut / Graphic Match**:
   - Transitioning from an illustration of an object (e.g. smartphone) directly to a real screenshot or zoomed UI of that exact object in the identical screen position.
5. **Snap Zoom / Whip Punch**:
   - A fast 0.20s digital push-in (scale 1.0 $\to$ 1.25) to emphasize a critical phrase or punchline.

### Prohibited Transitions
- Cheesy wipes (star, clock, diagonal wipe).
- Unmotivated cross-dissolves in high-energy dialogue (dissolves signal slow passage of time, which kills reel momentum).
- Extended black frame dips (creates the illusion the video has ended, causing the viewer to swipe away).

## 3. Audio & Dialogue Rhythm Rules

1. **Conversational Turn Latency**:
   - Pauses between distinct dialogue cues must strictly stay between **0.20s and 0.38s**.
   - Maximum allowable silence in entire reel: **0.50s** (only permissible for a major comic beat or shock cliffhanger).
2. **Music Sidechain Ducking**:
   - Background music must be attenuated by **-14 to -18 dBFS** whenever speech is active.
   - Attack time: 20ms. Release time: 150ms.
   - During brief speaker gaps (> 0.4s), music swells up +4 dB to maintain acoustic energy.
3. **Foley & Spot SFX**:
   - Every on-screen visual element entrance must have an acoustic accompaniment:
     - Pop / bubble: for icon and badge appearances.
     - Swish / whoosh: for slide-ins and camera whips.
     - Heavy sub-thud: for dramatic title drops or shocking statistics.
     - Ding / register chime: for success states, approvals, and reveals.

## 4. Retention & Engagement Engineering

1. **The 3-Second Rule**:
   - The viewer decides to stay or swipe in under 3 seconds.
   - The opening frame must display:
     - High-contrast visual question or absurdity.
     - Bold title text in the upper safe zone.
     - High-energy vocal statement (no long intros like *"Hello friends, today we will discuss..."*).
2. **Open Loops (Information Gaps)**:
   - Plant an unanswered question or mystery in Act 1 that is only resolved in the final 5 seconds.
3. **Pattern Interrupts**:
   - Every 10–12 seconds, disrupt the visual pattern: change background color, invert text styling, introduce a sound effect sting, or switch camera framing from medium to extreme closeup.
4. **Seamless Looping**:
   - The final spoken sentence connects grammatically or thematically into the first opening sentence, enticing viewers to rewatch the reel on loop.
